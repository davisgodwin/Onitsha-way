# v0.47.0 — Open life: the rest of stage 7, and stage 8 (the city responds; balance)

## Stage 7, second part: more ways to make a living
All from a new phone app, **Ventures**, and all done somewhere in the city. Adults only.
- **Event planner**: take bookings (traditional weddings, birthdays, child dedications, graduation parties, burial
  receptions). For each you choose the venue, the food and the music, and pay those suppliers up front. On the day, a
  canopy with chairs goes up at the place (on your map), 12:00–18:00. You run it, and there's always a problem to
  handle (the generator, rain, a late caterer). The client covers the suppliers up to the budget and pays your fee;
  good choices and good handling earn more, and going over budget costs you.
- **Interior decorator**: take a brief (a modern, classic or afro-chic living room, bedroom or shop front). Go to the
  client's house and choose three pieces: match their style and stay in their budget.
- **Tech agency**: register an agency (laptop needed), take client briefs (websites, online shops, apps, branding)
  and hire developers (daily wages). Work on the briefs yourself, deliver on time (late costs 30%), or the client
  cancels.
- **Landlord**: let a home you own to one of three applicants (rent and reliability differ). Rent comes in weekly,
  if they pay, with the odd repair bill. You can't move into a home while your tenant lives there.
- **Property developer**: buy one of the city's unfinished buildings near you, pay for the blockwork, the roof and the
  finishing (a few days each, with a signboard on site). When it's done, the finished building stands in the city.
  Sell it (a modest margin) or let it to a business.
- **Roadside mechanic**: take a call-out (inspection kit needed). A broken-down car with a warning triangle waits at
  the side of a road. Find the fault from the symptoms, fix it, get paid.
- **Security company**: register, hire and train guards, and take clients (an estate gate, a warehouse, a school, a
  plaza). Clients pay on Mondays and guards are paid daily. At night there are break-in attempts: trained guards stop
  most of them, and two that get through lose you the client. You can also post a guard at your own shop's door.
- **Street crew** (adults, abstract, no fighting): start a crew and work your turf (Upper Iweka park, the Main
  Market, the bridgehead, Ochanja). You can run a community watch, take loading fees, or collect a "levy" from
  traders: the levy is extortion, a crime the police open a case on. Influence fades every day, a rival crew holds
  what you don't, and you can talk peace with them.
- **Footballer and coach**: a trial at **Onitsha City FC** (fictional; fitness 3) puts you in a six-team league with
  the neighbourhood sides. Train on weekdays at the stadium. Play the league match on Saturdays in the football game:
  an appearance fee, plus a bonus for a win. The other fixtures are played out and there's a league table. Or coach
  the **Onitsha City Academy U-17s**: pick the tactics for Saturday and run training sessions, which make the side
  stronger.

## Stage 8: the city responds
- **The news**: written from what actually happened:
  - elections (the season opening, polling day, every result);
  - public projects opening, businesses opening and closing;
  - warrants and convictions;
  - your events, agency deliveries and buildings, and football results;
  - what's on today, and the big moments of your life (elected, graduated, promoted, signed).
  It's on the phone (The Courier) and on **three LED news screens** in town (by the mall, at the Main Market and at the
  bridgehead). Walk up to a screen to read it.
- **Wanted notices**: while the police or the EFCC have a warrant for you, **posters with your name** go up by the
  markets, the park and the bridgehead, and the screens turn red with the police notice. Strangers who recognise you
  may call it in (the case gets a little stronger), and police officers greet you differently.
- **Announcements**: the election season, polling day and the results reach the news and the screens.
- **People react to who you are**: strangers greet you as a well-known name, as an office holder ("Hon. …"), nervously
  if you're feared, coldly if you're disliked, and warily if you're wanted. Friends still greet you as friends.
- **Balance**: every honest job tops out below ₦8,000 an hour. The new ventures earn in the same range and carry their
  costs and risks (wages, suppliers, tenants who don't pay, late delivery). Crime pays more and is a crime. A test
  keeps these bands.
- **Fix**: work shifts were closed on **Mondays** instead of Sundays (the game's weekdays start on Monday). Now you
  can work Monday to Saturday, as intended; lectures are Monday to Friday.
- **Phones and performance**: the new phone apps were checked at phone size (390 × 844). The new buildings are a few
  merged meshes each.
